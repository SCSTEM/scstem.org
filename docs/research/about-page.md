# About-page reference research

Reviewed October 25, 2026. Seven official organization examples: two FIRST teams, three
community robotics programs, and two STEM nonprofits; five are in Pennsylvania. This is
content research for SC2's About-page rethink, not a design effectiveness study.

The brief values the Biohazard-to-SC2 founding story and historical photos, but reports that
chronological group pictures suggest shrinking membership and two separate photo scrollers,
the first ending in 2022, suggest activity stopped. Those are reported impressions, not
verified membership trends. The SC2 page audit and recommendations follow the reference notes.

## Observed examples

### 1. Miss Daisy / Team 341 — Ambler, PA; school FIRST team

[About our Team](https://www.team341.com/about-our-team/) opens with the after-school
program's purpose, activities, and eligibility: grades 9–12. An image accompanies the opening;
the extracted page does not establish its date. Activities include media and business as
well as engineering. Coaches/mentors and alumni have separate links; seasonal history has
its own navigation section. The About page also carries a dated March 2026 competition-news
link. The [homepage](https://www.team341.com/) supplies the 1999 founding date, school/location,
FIRST explanation, sponsor invitation, and latest-news route. History navigation stops at
2020 despite newer news: archive completeness and present activity are separate signals.

### 2. Team 254, The Cheesy Poofs — San Jose, CA; school FIRST team

[The Team](https://www.team254.com/about/) starts with a mascot image and seven paragraphs
of early history before program sections; “Today” and the mission appear near the bottom.
This is a counterexample to putting present identity first. The page distinguishes active
FTC participation, including 2025–26 results, from a VEX section explicitly dated 2009–2023
that explains the program transition. Leadership, mentors, photos, videos, blog, and
sponsorship are separate navigation destinations. Its useful pattern is explicit program
status; its long origin-and-awards narrative is not evidence that SC2 needs comparable length.

### 3. Krypton Robotics / Krypton Cougars — Hershey area, PA; multiple FIRST programs

The [homepage's About Us block](https://www.team2539.com/) identifies grades 1–12,
Dauphin and Lebanon counties, skills taught, and FLL/FTC/FRC in one short paragraph.
It follows with an embedded calendar, a “seasons of Krypton” section, sponsor/merch/donate
links, mentor/contact links, and the build-site address. Mentor and sponsor information
have separate destinations. The extracted text does not expose the seasonal images or
calendar events, so their recency cannot be assessed. Robot navigation ends at 2024:
neither that date nor a calendar embed alone establishes current participation.

### 4. Girls of Steel — Pittsburgh, PA; multiple FIRST programs

The [homepage](https://girlsofsteelrobotics.org/) leads with empowerment, program and
application links, then robotics, outreach, skills, mentor information, and practical FAQs.
Images accompany several of these sections. FAQs address location, application timing,
gender inclusion, and whether experience is required. The separate
[mission page](https://girlsofsteelrobotics.org/our-mission/) is brief; the
[programs page](https://girlsofsteelrobotics.org/about/our-programs/) organizes offerings
by grades and describes progression from FTC to FRC, with images beside program sections.
Navigation includes a 2026 open house. The homepage's “15 years” celebration and “16 Years”
metric disagree, while programs still list summer 2025 camps: current and stale signals
can coexist. Detailed founding history is not part of these pages' main narrative.

### 5. TechBrick Education — Harford County, MD; community FIRST nonprofit

[About Us](https://www.techbrickprograms.com/page/show/6593589-about-us) begins with
501(c)(3) status, K–12 education, and service area. After an image, one paragraph explains
the 2003 club, participation across school types, and the 2018 nonprofit formed to
administer the expanding program. Its wording still frames TechBrick “in 2021.” The
[registration homepage](https://www.techbrickprograms.com/) explains FIRST levels by
age, meeting locations, and a registration next step. This directly connects an original
club to an umbrella organization, but its undated “Registration is now open” statement
does not establish which season is accepting students. Read through indexed official-page
text because direct access returned 403.

### 6. STEM Coding Lab — Pittsburgh, PA; youth STEM nonprofit

[About Us](https://www.stemcodinglab.org/about-us) places an inspirational quotation before
a short origin story: the founding family/year, then a comparison between initial and
present reach. Mission and vision specify underserved K–8 children and economic opportunity.
The page continues through its rationale, board, staff, teachers, donors, school partners,
and other partners, with image entries accompanying people and organizations. A donation
CTA, nonprofit statement, and contact details close the page. This offers concrete identity
and accountability information, but the directory makes the overall page long. Reach
figures are the organization's undated claims, not independently verified outcomes.
Read through indexed official-page text because direct access timed out.

### 7. Da Vinci Science Center — Allentown, PA; STEM nonprofit

[Mission and History](https://www.davincisciencecenter.org/mission-and-history/) puts mission,
regional purpose, and families/schools before its origins. A compact history reaches the
2024 move, then links to a longer history. A separate short passage explains its namesake
and organizational merger. Images punctuate the page, and a clearly labeled 2019 digital
time capsule preserves older memories. Current exhibit links and membership/donation CTAs
follow. Navigation separates staff, impact, and annual reports/policies. This demonstrates
that a short founding explanation and an explicitly historical media collection can coexist
with routes to present offerings; a museum's ticketing and staffing needs differ from SC2's.

## Transferable lessons — interpretations to consider

These are editorial hypotheses for the parent audit, not measured usability or conversion
findings. Example numbers refer to the observations and source links above.

- **Establish the organization before its chronology.** A short statement of purpose,
  location, students served, and current activities appears in examples 1, 3, and 5.
  For SC2, distinguish the collective from the Biohazard team before telling their shared
  history. Grade bands and program links can carry detail, as in example 4.
- **Keep the founding connection, limit the initial history.** TechBrick's club-to-nonprofit
  paragraph and Da Vinci's summary-plus-history-link are relevant models (5, 7).
  A brief Biohazard-to-SC2 account could preserve the meaningful story while moving a fuller
  chronology to an explicitly labeled destination. Team 254 shows how much space a
  history-first approach consumes (2).
- **State present activity independently of the archive.** A dated recent activity and a
  maintained program/contact route can answer whether SC2 operates today (1, 4).
  Label closed programs and historical ranges explicitly (2). Avoid making the last
  archive photo, copyright year, or embedded calendar carry that responsibility (1, 3, 5).
- **Give photos a stated purpose.** Program-associated images (4) and a labeled time capsule
  (7) offer different roles for present participation and memories. Consider a recent
  activity photo plus a small, captioned founding selection, with further history available
  separately. This is a response to the brief, not proof that a particular gallery format
  works better. Group-photo headcounts cannot establish membership trends.
- **Keep next steps and accountability identifiable.** Program/application links (4),
  registration guidance (5), and support links (1, 3, 7) name concrete actions. Mentor links
  (1, 3, 4), nonprofit/contact details (5, 6), and reports (7) provide distinct trust
  information. A compact leadership summary with a deeper link is an option; reproducing
  the full directory in example 6 would add substantial length.

## Limits

This is a purposive sample, not a survey of typical practice. Findings describe official
page text, links, headings, and exposed image metadata; two examples use search-indexed
copies. No rendered mobile layouts, carousel behavior, image subjects, complete galleries,
or embedded calendars were verified. Image presence does not establish when a photograph
was taken. Search crawl dates are not publication dates. Organization claims about reach,
success, and inclusion were not audited. No analytics, conversion tests, or interviews
with prospective SC2 families were available; these examples support content options, not
claims that those options increase enrollment or donations.

## SC2 page audit and recommended direction

The local [About page](../../src/pages/about.astro) currently proceeds from the introduction through beginnings, the 2013–2022 photo scroller, the nonprofit's formation, the 2023-onward scroller, Biohazard today, outreach, future plans, and an invitation. Both photo sequences sort oldest first. The current organization and the relationship between its programs compete with a much larger chronological story.

The [photo component](../../src/components/ui/PhotoTimeline.astro) gives each image a caption, but the captions generally identify only the team and year. They do little to explain the significance of an individual moment. The user reports that the portraits suggest declining membership and that the first scroller's 2022 endpoint suggests inactivity. Those are reported impressions, not a membership trend measured from the images. A group photograph is not a reliable enrollment record.

Recommended editorial direction: introduce today's organization, show what students do, then tell a short origin story. Keep the historical photographs accessible through a clearly labeled archive.

1. **State the current identity early.** Explain that South Central STEM Collective is the Chambersburg nonprofit supporting Biohazard and FIRST LEGO League, serving students from Franklin County and beyond. Explain FIRST briefly in ordinary language. Put both current programs and their ages near the introduction, with links to their existing pages. Visitors should not have to read the founding history to understand how these names relate.
2. **Use a few photos with distinct jobs.** Prefer a current workshop or competition photo, a younger-student activity photo, and one archival image accompanying the origin story. Caption the action and verified date. Keep older images visibly historical; verify dates before describing an existing asset as recent. Avoid a chronological series of similarly framed group portraits on the main page, which invites comparisons of group size. Retain authentic current team photos in the archive.
3. **Replace both main-page scrollers.** Present the selected photographs directly in the ordinary page flow. The essential story and evidence of current activity should be visible without horizontal interaction. Preserve the full collection in one clearly labeled history destination, with the latest available season visible first. Existing Biohazard [season history](../../src/pages/programs/frc/robots.astro) already provides a home for detailed results and awards; a broader organization archive would also need to account for FIRST LEGO League.
4. **Condense the origin story to roughly 80–120 words.** Preserve Biohazard's 2012 start under 4-H, parents and mentors establishing SC2 in 2021, Biohazard's continuity, and the addition of FIRST LEGO League. Put pandemic details, the 2022 competition return, annual results, and complete team portraits in the history destination. These word counts are editorial targets, not research-derived thresholds.
5. **Use a small amount of concrete evidence about today.** Keep the fact that alumni return as mentors, a brief explanation of volunteer support and student supervision, and one or two recent outreach examples. The repository already records 2026 outreach. Keep facts scoped to the program they describe. Review the existing 10–20 students-per-season figure before reusing it as a claim about current enrollment.
6. **Shorten future plans and close with an invitation.** A sentence about expanding opportunities can connect to volunteering or sponsorship. Keep workshops and a permanent makerspace explicitly prospective. End with a primary Get involved action and a quieter support link.

A proposed reading order is: introduction and current programs; students and mentors in action; how Biohazard led to SC2; local community activity and next steps. Aim initially for about 350–500 words, plus captions, with three or four purposeful photographs. Retain warmth and the backstory while making the main page easy to skim.

Possible origin copy, based on the current local page:

> Our story began in 2012 with Biohazard, a high-school robotics team formed through Franklin County 4-H. In 2021, parents and mentors established South Central STEM Collective as an independent nonprofit to support the team and create more STEM opportunities. Biohazard continues today alongside our FIRST LEGO League teams for younger students. Together, they share a workspace in downtown Chambersburg, supported by students, families, and volunteer mentors.

If a separate archive is deferred, a smaller interim change is to move today's organization above the history and combine both scrollers into one gallery labeled with its complete date range, showing recent photos first. This addresses the false stopping point but still leaves the archive competing for attention on the main page; the curated-photo approach is preferred.

## Supporting usability research and validation

[Nielsen Norman Group's About Us research](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/) recommends a brief main-page summary with deeper organizational details available on supporting pages, and reports appreciation for understandable language and authentic photography. Its research covers varied organizations and is not a controlled test of SC2's audiences.

[W3C's carousel guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/carousels/) notes that carousel content can be difficult to discover. This supports keeping essential information visible outside a scroller; it does not establish that every carousel is inaccessible or ineffective.

Before implementing, update DESIGN.md §5's specific requirement for interleaved About-page timelines and assess whether its two-stage growth diagram still serves the condensed story. Use existing content collections for reusable history and photos.

Test a revised draft with a few people unfamiliar with SC2. After a brief look, ask what SC2 does, who can participate, how Biohazard relates to it, whether it appears active now, and what they would do next. Ask open questions before mentioning the reported concerns. These responses would validate the proposed structure more directly than copying another organization's page.

The SC2 audit above used local page source, collection entries, and selected image assets. A rendered local-browser review was not completed: the browser command encountered a missing pinned Node installation and a read-only tool-install location. No website code was changed for this research.
